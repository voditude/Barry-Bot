# Marketing & Management subject pack (light tier — see ../LIGHT-PACK-PROCESS.md)

Drafted from general subject knowledge, not iterated against a real course.

## Notation

No math notation by default (occasional metrics — CAC, LTV, basic management-accounting
figures — read as simple arithmetic, not dense formulas; treat heavier quantitative content
as economics-finance instead). The real "notation" here is applying a **named framework**
consistently (SWOT, Porter's Five Forces, the 4Ps, BCG growth-share matrix) — state the
framework's components once, in full, the first time it's introduced, the same proof-skeleton
pattern used elsewhere.

## Tooling

Framework diagrams (2x2 matrices, radial force diagrams) are conceptual/schematic, not data
plots — matplotlib can render simple grids and shapes but this hasn't been tested for this
use, unlike economics-finance's diagrams which are standard plot types. Flag this as an open
question for promotion to full tier, same as the engineering/CS diagram gaps above.

## Worked example shape

A case-study application: apply a named framework to a real or hypothetical company/scenario,
structured by the framework's own components (e.g. each of Porter's five forces, addressed in
turn) rather than free-form business commentary.

## Rigor / citation

Name the framework and, where conventionally attributed, its originator (e.g. "Porter's Five
Forces") the first time it's used, rather than presenting it as an unattributed general
observation.

## Tone note

Business writing conventionally favors punchy, direct, action-oriented language — this
aligns with `tone-guide.md`'s existing direct/no-filler rule more naturally than law's hedging
convention does; no exception needed here.
